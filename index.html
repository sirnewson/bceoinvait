Becoming CEO Live · Nairobi Personal Invitation

Dear Builder,

For years I have sat across from CEOs and asked them the question everybody wants answered: how did you actually do it? Not the highlight reel. The decisions, the doubt, the years nobody clapped for. Those conversations became Becoming CEO.

On Saturday, 12 September, we are taking that conversation off the screen and putting it in one room. No stage distance. No performance. Just a working class for people who are building something real and are serious enough about it to show up on a Saturday morning.

This is not a motivation session. You will leave with language for your business, a sharper view of your numbers, and a room of people who understand exactly what you are carrying. In my experience, the second thing is worth more than the first.

Seats are deliberately limited, because the value of this room is the room. If you have been waiting for the right moment to stop planning and start building in public. This is your invitation.

Come early. Bring your questions. Bring a friend who needs to hear it.

Philip Karanja
Host & Convener · Becoming CEO

Guest Speakers

The builders joining us

Two operators. Two completely different roads to the top seat: one built in leather and retail floors, one in hospitals and cancer wards. Both in the room on 12 September, unedited, and taking your questions.

Guest 01

Dennis Mwaura

Founder & CEO · Denri Africa

Dennis Mwaura turned Kenyan leather into a national retail brand. Denri Africa designs and makes bags, backpacks, luggage and corporate leather goods here at home, and has grown into 18 stores nationwide, from Nairobi to Kisii to Kitengela, with more than a million customers carrying the product. Nobody handed him the shortcut: it was built one branch, one production line and one price decision at a time. That is the conversation he brings to the room: supply, staffing, margins, and the discipline of opening the next store.

Guest 02

Dr. Maxwel Okoth, MBS

Group Managing Director · RFH Healthcare

A medical doctor who built a hospital group. Dr. Maxwel Okoth founded RFH Healthcare and has grown it into 11 branches, a Multispecialist Unit and the largest cancer centre in East Africa, with over 1,000 staff serving patients who had been priced out of quality care. With a Bachelor of Medicine and Surgery from the University of Nairobi and 13+ years in healthcare leadership, he builds public-private partnerships and sustainable care models. In 2024 the President awarded him the Moran of the Order of the Burning Spear (MBS).

  • 11Hospital branches
  • 1,000+Employees
  • #1Cancer centre in East Africa
MBS · 2024 Harvard SEP Yale SOM Stanford Seed Top 40 Under 40
Read full profile

The RFH Cancer Centre is a regional hub for advanced oncology, equipped with PET CT, SPECT CT, Lutetium Therapy, Gamma Knife and chemotherapy. Dr. Maxwel blends medical expertise with entrepreneurial acumen across corporate strategy, business expansion, stakeholder engagement and resource mobilisation, working with donors and regulators to get health projects actually implemented.

He is an alumnus of Harvard Business School’s Senior Executive Program, the Yale School of Management Global CEO programme, the Stanford Seed Transformation Program at Stanford Graduate School of Business, Managing Healthcare Business at Strathmore University, and the Kenya School of Government’s Senior Management and Public Administration Program.

His recognitions include the Moran of the Order of the Burning Spear (MBS) 2024 Presidential Award, Business Daily Top 40 Under 40 Men (2019), Top Male Entrepreneur Under 35, the A-254 Youth Entrepreneurship Award, Top 50 Most Influential Men 2024, Stanford Seed Leader of Most Improved Business 2024, and the KMA Entrepreneurship Award. Beyond healthcare, his portfolio spans insurance, pharmaceuticals, education, construction, media and financial services.
